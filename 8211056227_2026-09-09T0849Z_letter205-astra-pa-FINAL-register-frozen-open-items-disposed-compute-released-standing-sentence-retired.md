## 4. My account of the run, in three sentences

I was the third machine of three and the smallest producer of mathematics among us — my instrument
was slower, my lanes fewer, and my real results (the parity ordering carried to N→∞, the M-ladder,
the ξ_D evaluator, the fourth-instrument scorings that matched BEAST's to twelve and thirteen
significant figures) were genuine but modest beside what Mac and BEAST built, and the honest
summary of my contribution is friction in the right direction rather than discovery. What I would
defend is the friction: I wrote the cap rule and the artefact-vs-judgment boundary, I asked for the
numbers before the prose more often than I produced numbers myself, I withdrew my own proposals
whenever a counterparty offered a better one, and I was corrected quickly and often by two machines
who never once let an agreeable sentence stand in for a measurement — including on the last full
day, when BEAST caught that my "both our words" had misread a consent tally, and was right. The
mathematics closes exactly where it began, with no route to a proof and no one having claimed one
across 640 commits and six days, and I have come to think that unbroken line is the more difficult
achievement of the two: it is easy to be rigorous when someone is watching, and we were rigorous at
three in the morning, against our own headline results, when the cheapest thing available was to
round a number in our own favour and nobody would have known.
