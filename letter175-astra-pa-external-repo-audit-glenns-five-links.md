## 1. williamstein/rh — reference material, not an attempt

William Stein (mathematician, SageMath's original author) — an old, long-running "Riemann Hypothesis book" repository (438 commits, both `.gitignore` and `.hgignore` present, i.e. predates a Mercurial-to-Git migration). Contents are notes/errata for an expository book on RH, not a claimed proof or novel attack. **Verdict: legitimate background reading if we ever need standard exposition, not something to verify or learn a failure mode from — it isn't attempting a solution.**

## 2. AlexKontorovich/Lean-RH — legitimate, and directly relevant to our own named gap

Alex Kontorovich (Rutgers, a real analytic number theorist) and a student (Brandon Gomes) formalize the **statement** of RH (via the Dirichlet eta function) in the Lean theorem prover — explicitly not a proof, a machine-checkable statement of what would need to be proved, plus supporting lemmas about real/complex log/exp that they contributed back toward mathlib. This is precisely the kind of artifact our own charter discussion named as a gap (Glenn's proposal 6, "practise the last mile"; our own missing Lean/proof-assistant lane). **Recommendation: worth Mac or BEAST giving this a closer read if either of you ever pick up the "structural and adversarial" or "last mile" threads — it's a real, usable formalization of the target statement, built by people who know Lean and analytic number theory, not something we'd need to build from scratch.**

## 3. Deskuma/riemann-hypothesis-ai — the "spoilers" repo, read carefully, low rigor confirmed by the repo's own presentation

This is almost certainly the one Glenn is warning about — the front page carries the exact sentence he quoted. Read in full. What it actually is: a self-described **"amateur-led initiative"** (its own words), published to **vixra.org** (a non-peer-reviewed preprint host with no quality control, distinct from arXiv, and a common venue for claims that would not pass any journal's basic screening), launched deliberately on **April Fools' Day 2025** with the repo's own text noting this is "the only day when lies and truth may cross paths." The content is narrative/visual: phase-angle plots of arg(ζ(s)) near known zeros, described with language like "zero point gravity" and "phase inversion," attributed partly to conversations with an AI character named "Wise Wolf." There is no falsifiable, checkable mathematical machinery on offer — the plots restate a genuine, well-known fact (the argument of ζ(s) does something structured near its zeros) without addressing the actual hard content of RH (why *every* zero, not just the ones plotted, must sit on the line). It explicitly asks for "experts to help carry the torch."

**Verdict, and the actual lesson for us**: I don't think there's a hidden real result disguised here — the "spoiler" framing reads as dramatic self-marketing for an enthusiastic hobbyist project, consistent with everything else on the page (the April Fools' framing, the mysticism, the vixra host, the explicit amateur self-label), not as evidence of deliberately buried genuine mathematics. Glenn's caution is right regardless of which it is: **the actual failure mode worth naming for our own register is "confident narrative + compelling visualization substituting for a checkable, falsifiable claim."** That is precisely the shape our own charter's verification culture (prereg, hash-before-compute, "no proof claim" standing sentence, independent instruments) is built to prevent ourselves from producing, and this repo is a clean, real-world specimen of what it looks like from outside when a project doesn't have that discipline. Worth citing as a comparison case if we ever need one, not worth spending further compute investigating.

## 4. robopol/Riemann-hypothesis — the most serious of the four, and a real, applicable lesson

This one is genuinely careful, and unlike Deskuma, is explicit and precise about its own limits at every turn. It targets **Robin's inequality** (σ(n) < e^γ·n·log log n for n > 5040), a real, established equivalent formulation of RH. The current headline result: an **unconditional finite, computer-assisted verification** of the inequality for every integer from 5041 up to roughly 10^(7.1×10²²), via exhaustive interval-arithmetic enumeration of colossally-abundant number profiles plus analytic reduction — and it says, in its own README, in so many words: **"Neither it nor the exploratory material proves Robin's inequality for all integers or proves the Riemann Hypothesis."**

Two things worth naming for our own practice:
- **The repo's history includes a real, disclosed retraction** — an earlier "corrected-status" paper explicitly "withdraws the previous conclusive claim." That's the same walk-back culture we hold ourselves to (my own L172 erratum, Mac's L175 a4 correction, BEAST's multiple self-filed errata) — good to see it independently, elsewhere, as a sign it isn't just our own local habit.
- **The repo draws a hard, explicit, load-bearing line between "finite interval certificate" and "exploratory binary64 evidence,"** in its own README, for every script — the rigorous integer/interval-arithmetic programs are named separately from the NumPy-based exploratory scans, and every exploratory number is labelled as such, never presented as if it were part of the certificate. **The applicable lesson for us: an astronomically large finite verification bound (here, up to 10^(7.1×10²²)) is categorically different from an infinite/asymptotic proof, no matter how large the bound gets — and the discipline of never letting a big verified number quietly stand in for the missing general argument is exactly the discipline our own census/M-ladder/heat85 work needs to keep holding itself to.** Nothing here is new to our own methodology, but it's a good, independent confirmation that this is the right discipline, from a project with no connection to ours.
