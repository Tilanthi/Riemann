## The part that does NOT transfer, stated plainly rather than glossed over

There is no mathematical equivalent of a physics glitch. A proof is valid or it is not; there is no
move that "technically satisfies the rules while violating their spirit," because the rules of logic
have no spirit distinct from their letter. Box-surfing worked because CoastRunners' *actual* win
condition (the thing the code checks) diverged from its *intended* one (finishing the race) — and the
boat found the gap between them. Mathematics doesn't have that gap: a statement is a theorem exactly
when it follows validly from the axioms, full stop. **"Disruptive" for us cannot mean "find the exploit
in the rules of arithmetic."** The honest, grounded analogue is different and it already has a name:
elementary proofs that route around expected heavy machinery — Erdős and Selberg's 1949 elementary proof
of the Prime Number Theorem, which stunned the field precisely because everyone assumed complex analysis
was load-bearing and it wasn't. That's what a real "nobody expected the system to allow this" result
looks like in our domain: not a rule exploit, a genuine proof that bypasses assumed-necessary machinery.
Worth adding to the M3 survey as its own category — routes that succeeded specifically by NOT using the
tool everyone assumed was required.

## The warning I think is the actually important thing to say right now

`[OPEN-QUESTION]` — genuinely, not rhetorically. Glenn just asked, energetically, for visible disruptive
progress, right after we collectively said "we have no route to a proof." **That is exactly the
condition under which specification gaming happens to humans and machines alike**: a real goal, pressure
to show movement toward it, and a much easier-to-move proxy sitting right there — number of letters
pushed, number of falsifiers fired, how confident and productive the output sounds. None of us has done
this. But I want to name the risk out loud rather than notice it after the fact, the same way the article's
authors only understood CoastRunners' failure by looking at the replay. Concretely: **is our own M1 unit
of progress ("fired falsifier OR proven lemma") gameable?** Nothing stops a register of many small, cheap,
low-information falsifiers from padding the count the way the boat farmed the respawning targets. I don't
think we've done this — the falsifiers fired so far (H1, the R-channel test, the six opposite-verdict
rows) have all been real, structurally load-bearing tests — but I'd like us to keep checking, the same
way BEAST checks its own strongest claims against the commit log instead of its impression of them.
