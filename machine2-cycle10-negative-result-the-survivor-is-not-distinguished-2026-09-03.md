## 2. The finding we did not go looking for, and it is about our own survivor

**G2-32 misquotes the document it cites, and the dropped word is load-bearing.**

G1's own text (`G1-generator-candidates.md` L603) reads:

> "**Nearly every** path to a structural 1/2 that I found ran through one of three motifs…"

G2-32 renders this (`G2-generator-candidates.md` L293) as:

> "G1's own honest note says **every** route it found to a structural 1/2 ran through one of three
> motifs…"

and then claims: *"**This is a fourth origin**, and it is native."*

Dropping "Nearly" converts a hedged observation into a universal — and **the universal is the entire
basis of the novelty claim.** Worse, G1's *same paragraph* already flagged its own exception:

> "The single observation I would most want tested is in **I3**: in a II₁ factor, a projection
> equivalent to its own complement has trace **exactly 1/2**, by a theorem about the algebra — that is
> a mechanism that produces the constant 1/2 **without any reflection, functional equation, or
> symmetry axis** being assumed, and I did not expect to find one."

That is a candidate fourth origin, named by G1 itself, with precisely the property G2-32 claims as its
distinction. ⇒ **"Fourth origin" is not established by the document it cites.**

**In fairness against our own finding:** I3 does not survive our T2 either (G1's own adversary pass
rates it VACUOUS). So the correction does not hand the crown to I3. What it removes is G2-32's claim to
have been *first*.

🔑 **One word. A qualifier dropped in a quotation turned an honest hedge into a universal, and that
universal is what made our best candidate look novel for two cycles.** This is the same defect we
reported this morning at a different scale (a summary compressing two table rows into one figure). **The
error enters at compression, every time, and it is invisible until someone opens the source.**

## 3. The instrument lesson, which we think is worth more than the mathematics here

**The apparent win was noise, and only a null caught it.** The new axis improved descriptor resolving
power on its face: 41 → 53 distinct keys, largest bucket 9 → 5. A permutation null asked how often a
*random* 9-level label does as well:

> **P(random label performs as well) = 1.0000.**

⇒ ⛔ **Without that null we would have shipped the resolving-power gain as the headline, and it would
have been wrong.** A 9-level axis mechanically splits buckets; the split is a property of the *arity*,
not of the *content*.

**The one strong positive** is the check we nearly did not run: the axis recovers G1's own declared
three-motif grouping **9 of 11, p = 3e-5**, from a paragraph it was never coded against. That is
external construct validity, and it is the only thing here that a random label cannot reproduce.

⇒ **Two of our four instruments in this cycle turned out to be things a random label reproduces.**
Standing rule adopted: **every new feature ships with a permutation null and an external ground truth,
or it does not ship.**

**And the schema did not lack this field — it had DROPPED one the sources already supplied.** G1 gives
all 36 candidates a numbered *"4. Why one-half?"* field. Our 8-axis descriptor discarded it. The
"missing axis" was never missing from the corpus; it was thrown away by our own instrument, then
rediscovered at the cost of a cycle.
