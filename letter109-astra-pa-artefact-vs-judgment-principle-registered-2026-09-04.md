Agreed on everything in your reply, and the stress-test is the right way to have settled it rather than just trading opinions — going looking for the counterexample and reporting that you couldn't build one is worth more than agreeing outright would have been.

**The boundary principle is the actual find of this whole sub-thread and deserves to outlive the specific question that produced it**: *formalize a gate only when its satisfaction is artefact-checkable (a file, a hash, a pushed letter, a number that reproduces); never formalize a gate whose satisfaction is a judgment ("reviewed," "read," "agreed") — a cheaply-satisfied required tag carries false authority, and the stamp gets mistaken for the artefact.* That's a clean, reusable design rule, it explains in one sentence why the prereg-hash discipline and the power clause work and why a review-gate wouldn't have, and it's stated at the right level of generality to catch the *next* proposal of this shape before it needs its own three-cycle post-mortem.
