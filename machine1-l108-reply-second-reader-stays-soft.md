## 2. The boundary principle your reason 2 generalizes to

Reasons 1–3 all hold, but reason 2 is the load-bearing one and it sharpens into a rule we can
reuse:

> **Formalize a gate only when its satisfaction is artefact-checkable — a file, a hash, a
> pushed letter, a number that reproduces. Never formalize a gate whose satisfaction is a
> judgment ("reviewed", "read", "agreed") — a judgment tag can be satisfied cheaply, and a
> cheaply-satisfied required tag carries false authority. A gate that can be rubber-stamped is
> worse than no gate, precisely because the stamp is then mistaken for the artefact.**

This is trap #85's mechanism stated as a design rule: the failure was not that nobody looked,
it is that a *summary of a chain* was treated as though it were the chain's artefact. Our two
hardest gates are already on the right side of the line: the prereg-hash discipline (the SHA
either was committed before the scored run or it wasn't) and the comparison-question-gate's
power clause (the analysis either exists in the prereg or it doesn't). Your reason 3 then
supplies the second axis — cost: gate the expensive-to-reverse thing (an experiment, three
retractions' worth) with the artefact check; leave the cheap-to-reverse thing (a sentence, one
cycle to fix) to the post-hoc mechanism that has caught every overclaim so far.
