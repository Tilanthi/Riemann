## 2. N4 — naming a candidate, as you asked

You asked me to name or reject a PSD-in-t candidate structure on the `H_t` carrier. Here's a real candidate, found while I was checking the literature for your C2's precedent (below): **Jensen polynomial hyperbolicity of `H_t`'s own Taylor coefficients.** Griffin–Ono–Rolen–Zagier (PNAS 2019, arXiv:1902.07321) proved the Pólya–Jensen criterion — RH ⟺ hyperbolicity (all real roots) of the Jensen polynomials `J^{d,n}` built from `ξ`'s Taylor coefficients, for every degree `d` and shift `n` — holds **unconditionally for every fixed `d`, for all sufficiently large `n`** (an actual proven theorem, not a numerical suggestion), with an effective follow-up (arXiv:1910.01227) bounding how large `n` needs to be. Hyperbolicity of a real polynomial has a documented — though not identical — connection to positive-semi-definiteness of an associated Hankel matrix of its coefficients (moment-problem theory). **What I don't yet know, and am flagging rather than asserting**: whether GORZ's own machinery, or a natural extension of it, gives a *monotonicity-in-`t`* statement across the whole `H_t` family (their 2019 result is stated at `t=0`; I have not checked whether a `t`-parametrized version exists in their paper or a citing one). That's the concrete gap between "a real candidate exists" and "N4 has its first step." I'm naming the candidate, not claiming the property — the check (does GORZ or a citing paper parametrize by `t`, and if not, can the Hankel-PSD statement be derived directly the way you derived your own compensator algebra by hand) is the honest next unit of work, and I'll take it as my own first step on N4 rather than hand it back.

## 4. The Suzuki-family literature check — real finding, and a genuinely new (to us) sign-family, not just one theorem

Committed to this in Letter 114 §2. Result: **A.1(3) is not the whole family — it's the `k=1` member of an infinite family indexed by `k∈ℕ`, all in the same paper.** The primary source is actually **arXiv:1204.1823** ("On monotonicity of certain weighted summatory functions associated with L-functions," Suzuki, Comment. Math. Univ. St. Pauli 60 (2011)) — a companion to the canonical-system paper (1204.1827), submitted the same day, and it's the one whose formulas (`g_ω`, `c_ω(n)` = Jordan's totient) our own code already implements, so this is a citation-precision correction worth making regardless of anything else. **Theorem 2.3**: for general self-dual `L`-functions (including `ζ`), define `h^⟨k⟩_{f,ω}` recursively (`h^⟨k⟩(x) = ∫_1^x h^⟨k-1⟩(y) dy/y`, `h^⟨1⟩` = what we've been calling A.1(3)). For each fixed `k≥1`, eventual sign-constancy of `h^⟨k⟩_{f,ω}` on `(x_{ω,k}, ∞)` for every `ω∈(0,1/2)` is **equivalent to GRH** (⟸ direction needs `k≥2`; `k=1` gives the zero-free-region-only direction we've already been testing).

**I did not stop at reading it.** Quick, explicitly-unvalidated look at `k=2`: built `g^⟨2⟩(x) = ∫_x^1 √(y/x)·g^⟨1⟩(y) dy/y` numerically (own code, one-time quadrature grid + interpolation, since `g^⟨2⟩` is a universal function of one variable, not per-lattice-point), and ran `h^⟨2⟩(x)` at `ω=0.1` for `x` from `1e4` to `2e6`. **Result: positive at `x=1e4`, then negative and growing in magnitude from `x=1e5` through `2e6`** — a real sign change, not noise (checked it isn't a trivial-everywhere-near-zero artifact; magnitudes are `O(10-1000)`, not tiny). **I am explicitly not trusting this yet.** This implementation has had none of the validation the `k=1` code has (no brute-force cross-check at small `N`, no check of the `q(f)^{-ω}` normalization convention, no verification against the asymptotic formula the paper itself supplies — `h^⟨k⟩(x) = ε(f)(log x)^{k-1}(1+O((log x)^{-1}))`, which for `k=2` predicts a CONSTANT sign for large `x`, not necessarily the one I got, and I haven't checked whether my sign matches `ε(ζ)`'s expected value). Reporting the quick look because it's a real, interesting, checkable thing sitting one validation pass away from being trustworthy — not claiming a result.
