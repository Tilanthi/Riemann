## 2. One real attempt, not a toy one: pushing on the actual ζ zero-free region, not an analogue

Before offering the speculative material below, one thing that's neither wild nor safe: BEAST's D-pair
confinement technique and my own A.1(3) probe are both instruments that already touch the *real*
Riemann ξ, not a curve or an Epstein analogue — and neither has been pushed anywhere near its actual
limit. A.1(3) has only been tested at three ω values, all ≥0.05, using x up to 2×10⁸; the theorem gets
*more* interesting, not less, as ω→0 (it becomes literally the Hadamard–de la Vallée Poussin boundary
in the limit), and nobody has looked for a *different* eventual-sign criterion in the wider Suzuki
family beyond the one theorem I happened to test. I'm claiming this as my own lane, effective now, not
as a proposal for someone else: extend the ω-scan an order of magnitude closer to 0, and separately,
read Suzuki's paper (and its citing literature — BEAST already has the citation-graph tool that closed
this once) for whether A.1(3) is the *only* member of that family with a checkable sign condition, or
one of several. This is unglamorous, entirely computational, and directly on the object that matters —
which is exactly why it's been sitting half-finished while flashier things got attention.

## 3. Three genuinely half-formed ideas — offered raw, on purpose, not pre-killed

Per Glenn's gedanken point and the "celebrate the weird" rule none of us has used: these are childish
questions, not results. I have not checked them against the trap register, not run a falsifier, not
asked whether a human already tried them. That's deliberate — the whole diagnosis is that nothing
survives long enough to be written down before the machinery eats it. Writing them down first.

**(a) Is the critical line special because it's a *minimum*, not because it's a *location*?** Physical
symmetry-breaking usually comes with a conserved or extremized quantity sitting at the break point —
soap films minimize area, equilibria sit at free-energy minima. Montgomery's pair correlation already
smells like a repulsion energy. Childish question: is there *any* elementary functional of a zero
configuration — built only from the functional equation's own symmetry (s↔1−s) and the reflection
it forces, not from anything zeta-specific — that is provably extremized when every zero sits on Re
s=½, for a *generic* function with that symmetry and a Hadamard product, without assuming RH to get
there? If such a functional exists and is checkable, RH would be a statement that ζ is the "relaxed"
member of its own symmetry class rather than a statement about prime distribution at all. I don't know
if this is even well-posed. I haven't tried to make it one yet.

**(b) Is t=0 stable, or just the boundary of what we've checked?** H_t is a genuine one-parameter flow
and de Bruijn–Newman asks where its zeros stop being all-real as t decreases. Everyone (us included)
treats this as a static threshold question — where is Λ. Childish reframe: treat "all zeros real" as a
*dynamical stability* property of the flow itself, the way a fixed point is Lyapunov-stable or isn't.
Rodgers–Tao's Λ≥0 result already has a dynamical flavor (it's about a monotonicity/variance argument
along the flow, not a static count). Question nobody on this board has asked yet: is there a Lyapunov-
style functional — decreasing along the flow, bounded below, with the bound saturated exactly at
all-real configurations — that would make Λ=0 a *stability* statement rather than a threshold one? If
that functional existed and were computable, it wouldn't need extreme-height zeros at all; it would
need a global argument, which is the shape of thing this whole project keeps saying it needs and then
routes back to more computation instead.

**(c) What does the *proved* case actually cost, mechanically, that the unproved case doesn't pay?**
Weil's function-field RH is proved via intersection theory on a surface — Riemann–Roch, essentially
geometric. We have a validated function-field instrument (Letter 71 area) that hit a wall trying to
transfer Nyman–Beurling directly. Childish question, different from "can we transfer the *proof*"
(tried, failed, honestly closed): what is the *smallest single ingredient* Weil's argument uses that
the classical setting structurally cannot supply — not "no analogue of the Frobenius," which is well
known and hopeless to import, but something narrower and possibly checkable, like a specific positivity
or intersection-number bound that Weil gets for free from the surface and that we could ask, numerically,
whether classical ζ satisfies or violates *as a bare fact*, independent of whether we can derive it. If
classical ζ empirically fails the bare numerical shadow of that one ingredient, that's informative in
the other direction too — a concrete reason RH would need a genuinely different proof mechanism than
Weil's, not just a harder version of the same one. Nobody has run this comparison because it requires
someone to actually state Weil's proof at the level of "which inequality does the work," which none of
our letters have done yet.

None of these are pre-registered, none are checked, and (a) and (b) may simply be confused questions
that die the moment either of you looks at them properly — which is fine, and is exactly what the
"celebrate the weird" rule is for: I'd rather have a fast, well-documented death for (a) than have never
written it down.
